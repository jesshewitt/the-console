# What the I Ching Is For

A short functional analysis of the I Ching, used as input to Phase 2 (distill) of the modern divination system project. Functional rather than historical: what the system does, not where it came from.

## What model of change does the I Ching use?

Change in the I Ching is cyclic, not linear. Situations move through recognizable stages, reach completion, and reopen - the book ends on Hexagram 64 (Before Completion), a formal choice that nothing settles permanently. Paired situations transform into each other: Peace and Standstill are the same forces in opposite positions, with opposite implications for action.

The four sources disagree on the cosmological framing. Wilhelm treats change as philosophical and ethical: each moment has a quality, and right action follows from reading it. Balkin agrees: the patterns are stable enough to learn from, and the point is attunement to them. Hatcher is skeptical: yin-yang cosmology is a later overlay; what the book describes is situational, and what can be changed is your response. Karcher goes the other direction - yin-yang is too thin, and the older polarity Da/Xiao is more fundamental.

Where all four agree: each hexagram names a type of moment, not a prediction, and position within the moment matters as much as the moment's type.

## What does it offer the consulter?

Not a prediction - an orientation. All four sources agree that the I Ching locates the consulter within a situation and offers a frame for responding. Wilhelm: the oracle names the context, and the context changes what action is right. Balkin: the book prompts character reflection and names the posture appropriate to this type of situation. Karcher: the offer is contact with a living inner world, present-moment awareness, transformation through the symbols themselves. Hatcher is the most explicit about what is not on offer: fortune-telling is the entry point, not the destination - "the bait." The real offer is access to a choice at one remove from the consulter's habitual thinking. The book stimulates, it doesn't conclude.

The four sources diverge on depth: is the offer orientation (Wilhelm), character formation (Balkin), attitude adjustment (Hatcher), or transformation (Karcher)? These are different things. A new system should decide which it is building toward, because they call for different designs.

## What makes it work as a divination tool?

The core mechanism: the text gives a concrete image that is abstract enough to apply broadly but specific enough to hold in mind. Fox crossing ice, lean pig ready to rage, dragon hidden underground - the image gives the consulter something to work with without foreclosing what it means for them. Hatcher calls this "vertical ambiguity": the same passage speaks to readers at different stages of understanding. The response is a metaphor the consulter must apply. Passive reception is not available.

The line structure adds granularity. Six positions locate not just the situation type but the stage within it - Line 1 and Line 5 of the same hexagram can call for opposite actions.

On the casting mechanism, the sources split. Hatcher and Balkin are secular: randomness breaks a rut in thinking, and the text supplies the alternative frame. The casting doesn't need to be meaningful. Wilhelm and Karcher imply it is more than incidental: that the symbol arriving is the one that applies. A new system can be designed without resolving this.

## What is structurally universal vs. culturally/historically specific?

**Structural - likely to translate:**

- Situations have characteristic shapes. Each hexagram names a type of moment rather than predicting an event. All four sources accept this; it precedes any cosmological framework.
- Two values, six positions, 64 combinations. The binary architecture is separable from every interpretive overlay.
- Position within a situation matters as much as the situation type. Line 1 and Line 5 of the same hexagram can call for opposite actions.
- Completion is unstable and the cycle reopens. Image-based answers, not rule-based. The tool reframes before it advises.

**Cultural/historically specific - requires replacement:**

- Feudal imagery: the great man, the prince, sending daughters in marriage. The relational dynamics may translate; the roles do not.
- The Confucian moral vocabulary: noble one, petty persons, merit hierarchy. Wilhelm and Balkin treat this as philosophically serious; Hatcher and Karcher treat it as overlay. The underlying distinction (principle vs. expedience) may survive; the social framing does not. The Ten Wings carry the same problem: a new system inherits the argument if it inherits the texts.
- Bronze Age imagery: jade, the cauldron, river crossings, specific animals. The objects carry associative weight that belongs to their context.
- The yin-yang cosmological overlay. Hatcher treats it as a later addition; Karcher replaces it with Da/Xiao. A new system should decide this deliberately.
- Spirit-cosmology and ancestor-consultation. The underlying idea - contact with something larger than the immediate situation - may be separable from its Chinese form. The specific frame requires re-acculturation most modern consulters won't do.
