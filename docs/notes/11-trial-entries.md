## Trial trigram entry: WATCH

# WATCH (☵)

**Pattern.** closed / focused / inward (bottom: closed, middle: focused, top: inward)

**Core sense.** Watchful attention on one source, awaiting return.

**Description.** A channel that exists - tuned to a specific place, ready to receive - but isn't carrying energy right now. The work isn't sending and isn't taking in; the work is the watching itself. You're holding the channel open in one direction, attentive, expectant. Something will arrive, and you're configured to catch it when it does.

This is the posture of vigil. It isn't passive in the way pure rest is - a closed, distributed, inward channel (ROOM) is generally available without object; WATCH has a specific object. There's a *where* the attention is pointed, even if no traffic is currently flowing.

**Range.**
- *Inner life*: waiting for an answer to surface in your own thinking
- *Relationships*: waiting for a response from someone specific
- *Work*: awaiting feedback on submitted work; the result of a long-running process
- *Body*: holding still, attentive to a specific sensation about to arrive
- *World*: watching for one event to happen (an announcement, a development)

**In combination.** When WATCH meets another channel state, it brings expectant readiness to the meeting. Whatever the other state is doing, this one is waiting for what it points at.

## Trial hexagram entry: WATCH → POUR

(lower trigram WATCH, upper trigram POUR; the present channel is WATCH, the emerging channel is POUR)

# Hexagram XX (☰ over ☵)

**Composition.**
- *Lower (present)*: WATCH (closed / focused / inward)
- *Upper (emerging)*: POUR (open / focused / outward)

**Core sense.** Watchful waiting becoming concentrated action.

**Image / situation.** You've been holding watchful attention on one specific thing - listening for an answer to surface, waiting for a particular message, configured to respond when a certain signal arrives. The waiting hasn't been idle; it's been a posture, a held readiness. Now the waiting is ending. What you've been watching for is arriving, or the time to wait passes; either way, the held attention finds its target and the channel opens, pouring outward in concentrated action. The vigil resolves into the deed it was prepared for.

## What worked, what didn't

**What worked**:
- The substrate's channel-state descriptions gave full bones for the trigram entry (name, pattern, core sense, description, range, in-combination). I didn't invent anything not derivable from substrate Section 3.
- The temporal hexagram structure (present → emerging) made the hexagram entry compose naturally. The "trajectory" framing is doing real work; I could see what kind of entry to write because the worldview said "this is a movement from channel A to channel B."
- Voice came out right: present-tense, image-grounded, no jargon, no prediction. The style guide section was sufficient guidance.

**What was harder**:
- The hexagram entry needed me to articulate *why* WATCH might become POUR (the trajectory's narrative logic). The substrate doesn't say "WATCH naturally precedes POUR" or anything like that - I had to find a coherent reading for this particular pair. This is OK as a creative writing task per entry, but it does mean the entry writer is doing real work *per hexagram* to find the trajectory's logic. Not a substrate gap exactly, but a reminder that 64 entries × this much per-entry thought is real Phase 4 work.

**Substrate gap surfaced**: hexagram numbering convention. The substrate doesn't specify how hexagrams are numbered (1-64? 0-63? indexed by binary value of upper-lower or lower-upper?). I left it as "Hexagram XX" in the trial. Need to either decide now or explicitly defer to Phase 4.
