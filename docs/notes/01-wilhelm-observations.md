## 1. What model of change does the I Ching use?

- Change is cyclic, not linear. Hex 24 (Return) says explicitly: "Return is grounded in the course of nature. The movement is cyclical. The way is closed in itself." The seventh stage of any movement brings a return to the beginning. The book's own ending - Hex 64 (Before Completion) following Hex 63 (After Completion) - enacts this.
- Change proceeds through paired opposites that transform into each other rather than cancel out. Hex 1 and 2 are "counterpart, not opposite; completion, not adversary." When all lines of Hex 1 become nines it transforms into Hex 2, and vice versa. The poles generate each other.
- Each moment has a characteristic quality that determines what action is appropriate. Hex 11 (Peace) and Hex 12 (Standstill) are the same forces in inverted positions, with opposite implications for how to behave. Completion is unstable and already contains its own undoing (Hex 63: "Precisely when perfect equilibrium is reached, every motion can lead to disintegration arising again").
- Small beginnings carry large consequences. Hex 44 warns that one dark line entering from below must be checked at once; Hex 2 Line 1 notes that hoarfrost underfoot means solid ice is near. The system is oriented toward early recognition of direction.

## 2. What does it offer the consulter?

- Orientation within time - an answer to "where am I in the pattern?" rather than a prediction of what will happen. Hex 24 says "on the seventh day comes Return" as a structural fact about cycles, not a promise about a specific outcome.
- A quality assessment of the moment rather than a prescription. The same general situation (withdrawal from public life) is the right response to Standstill (Hex 12) and the wrong response during Peace (Hex 11). Context changes the action; the oracle names the context.
- A prompt for self-examination. Line commentaries repeatedly turn the question inward. Hex 24 Line 4: "one should not hide behind petty excuses, but take counsel with oneself and examine oneself." Hex 44's warning about underestimating small temptations applies to external circumstances and inner ones alike.
- Guidance on the quality of waiting. Hex 64 Line 1 commentary: patience "must not be a sluggish waiting that lives from day to day. One must form within oneself the strength that enables one to advance." The I Ching distinguishes active from passive waiting; both can be right or wrong depending on time.

## 3. What makes it work as a divination tool?

- Each hexagram describes a situation type, not a fixed event. The descriptions are abstract enough to cover a wide range of circumstances. Hex 44's "brazen maiden who gives herself away" is explicitly interpreted as any underestimated temptation or small encroachment that gains power by being ignored.
- The line structure adds granularity. Six positions locate not just the situation type but the stage within it. Hex 1's lines trace a complete arc: hidden potential, early activity, full engagement, transition, mastery, overreach, and productive union - a reader's entry point can be anywhere in that arc.
- Tension is built into every positive situation. Hex 11: "No plain that is not followed by a slope, no going that is not followed by return." Even favorable readings require continued attention. This prevents the tool from being merely flattering.
- The images are concrete enough to spark association without over-determining meaning. Fox crossing ice (Hex 64), lean pig ready to rage (Hex 44), dragon hidden underground (Hex 1 Line 1). The specificity gives the consulter something to hold while thinking; it does not foreclose what applies to them.

## 4. What is structurally universal vs. culturally specific?

**Likely to translate:**
- The core model: situations have qualities; those qualities change through recognizable stages; your role is to read where you are and respond accordingly.
- Position within a situation matters as much as the situation type. The line structure carries this idea - the same force at Line 1 vs. Line 5 calls for different behavior (demonstrated across Hex 1, 44, 63).
- Completion does not mean rest. The book ending on Hex 64 (Before Completion) is a structural claim: the cycle re-opens. This is a transferable insight about ongoing processes.

**Requires translation or replacement:**
- Social structures used as analogies: king and minister, sending one's daughter in marriage (Hex 11 Line 5), colonial warfare (Hex 63 Line 3). The underlying relational dynamics may translate; the specific roles do not.
- The seasonal and directional correspondences coded into trigrams (northeast = late winter, northwest = late autumn, etc.) depend on a specific geography and cosmology.
- The moral vocabulary of "petty persons" vs. "noble one" carries Confucian class assumptions. The underlying distinction - acting from principle vs. acting from expedience - may be reusable, but the social framing needs rethinking.
- Specific animal symbols (horse, dragon, fox, lean pig) carry Chinese associative weight. A new system could use animals as image-carriers but would need to build or borrow their associations deliberately.
