# Phase 3 Task 2: The Two Primary States

## Decision: On / Off

The two primary states of the system. Computer-binary register: simplest, most universally understood, and the underpinning of electronics. Anyone can read "on" and "off" without explanation.

## Definitions

**On.** The state of being engaged, present, declared, in-play. The position has substance. Something is happening here - a force is asserting itself, an aspect of the situation is active.

**Off.** The state of being disengaged, latent, unstated, at rest. The position has space. The force is held back, the aspect is dormant, the room is left open.

## Range of application

Both states apply across domains. A position that's *on* in one register and *off* in another is normal; the position itself is what's binary, not the situation as a whole.

- *Inner life*: an attention, an impulse, a feeling - on or off
- *Relationships*: an honesty, an affection, a withdrawal - on or off
- *Work*: a project, an effort, a focus - on or off
- *World events*: a power, a movement, a public attention - on or off
- *Embodied life*: a tension, a relaxation, a held breath - on or off

## What these states are NOT

- *Not yang/yin.* The gendered cosmology of the original is explicitly rejected. On/off carries no sex, no temperament, no inherent moral weight.
- *Not good/bad.* On is not virtuous; off is not failure. Both are necessary; neither is preferred.
- *Not strong/weak, real/unreal, full/empty.* Connotations of magnitude or substance are not built in.
- *Not "on means active in a moral sense."* On just means present. A cruelty can be on; a kindness can be off.
- *Not "off means absent."* Off is at rest, latent, holding space. The off state is a real state, not the absence of state.

## Interpretive frames (for later)

The on/off binary is the technical foundation. Multiple interpretive frames can be layered over it in entry text and in the system's "How to read" material, without any single frame being load-bearing in the substrate. Examples of frames the entries may draw from:

- *Signal / Silence* - on as declared information, off as the receiving room
- *Charge / Ground* - on as electrical potential, off as the reference state
- *Pulse / Pause* - on as beat, off as the gap between
- *Present / Absent* - on as in-the-room, off as elsewhere
- *Active / Latent* - on as expressing, off as held in reserve
- *Spoken / Unspoken* - on as voiced, off as the unsaid

These frames are *readings*, not definitions. The substrate names the binary as on/off; the entry writers can reach for whichever frame fits the situation an entry is about. This keeps the substrate plain while letting the surface text carry varied imagery.

## Notation in entries

When an entry needs to reference the binary structurally, *on* and *off* are used directly: "three positions of *on* below, three positions of *off* above." When an entry is doing image work, the chosen frame can be used without naming the underlying binary explicitly: "the pulse is at the base, the pause receives it from above" - the reader hears the image; the structure is implicit.
