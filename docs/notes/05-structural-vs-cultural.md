# Structural vs. Cultural: What Translates from the I Ching

Synthesized from: 01-wilhelm, 02-hatcher, 03-karcher, 04-balkin.

The four sources don't fully agree on where the line falls. Disagreements are noted below rather than averaged out.

---

## Structural (likely universal)

**Situations have characteristic shapes.** Each hexagram names a type of moment - not a prediction, but a description of the forces in play. All four sources accept this. It precedes any cosmological framework.

**Binary primitive states composing into complex situations.** Two values, six positions, 64 combinations. Hatcher treats this architecture as load-bearing, separable from every interpretive overlay. The math doesn't depend on Chinese cosmology.

**Mutating states (the changing-line mechanism).** A situation contains the seeds of the next one. Changing lines mark which parts of a moment are in transition - a structural claim about how moments move, not a cultural one.

**Position within a situation matters as much as the situation type.** Line 1 and Line 5 of the same hexagram can call for opposite actions. The idea translates directly to any layered situation model.

**Completion is unstable; the cycle reopens.** The book ending on Hex 64 (Before Completion) is a formal choice: nothing settles permanently. It's a structural claim, not a cosmological one.

**Image-based, not rule-based answers.** The response is a metaphor the consulter applies, not a conclusion they receive. All four sources depend on this. The gap between image and application is what makes it work - portable to any oracle design.

**The tool reframes before it advises.** Balkin: when linear thinking has stalled, the oracle supplies an alternative frame. The value comes from interruption, not prediction. This function is structural, not specific to the I Ching.

## Cultural / historically specific

**Feudal and court imagery.** "The great man," "the prince," sending one's daughter in marriage, colonial warfare. Wilhelm keeps this vocabulary; Hatcher strips it. The relational dynamics beneath these roles may translate - the roles themselves do not.

**Strict gendered cosmology (yin/yang as feminine/masculine).** Hatcher rejects yin-yang as a later overlay. Karcher relies instead on an older polarity (Da/Xiao) that he treats as more fundamental. The disagreement is real: Karcher needs some version of the polarity as a universal; Hatcher does not. A new system should decide this deliberately.

**The Confucian moral vocabulary.** Junzi (the noble one), petty persons, merit hierarchy. Wilhelm and Balkin treat this as philosophically serious. Hatcher and Karcher treat it as historical overlay. The underlying distinction - acting from principle vs. expedience - may be reusable; the social framing is not.

**The Ten Wings as authoritative.** Wilhelm and Balkin treat them as integral to any serious reading. Hatcher warns against them as distorting additions. A new system can't inherit the Ten Wings without inheriting the argument about them.

**Bronze Age material imagery.** Jade, the cauldron, specific animals (fox, dragon, lean pig), river crossings, sacrificial rituals. These were already metaphors in the original text, so the layer is thin - but the specific objects carry associative weight that belongs to their context. A new system should build its own image vocabulary.

**Ancestor-consultation and spirit-cosmology.** Karcher argues the shamanic layer (shen/gui) points at universal experiences - inspiration, trauma, unresolved patterns. Even granting that, the specific ancestor-and-spirit frame requires re-acculturation most modern consulters won't do. The underlying idea (contact with something larger than your immediate situation) may be separable from its Chinese form.

**The King Wen sequence as authoritative ordering.** The arrangement of the 64 hexagrams carries traditional meaning (pairs, reversals, opposites) but is also a historical artifact. A new system need not reproduce or explain it.
